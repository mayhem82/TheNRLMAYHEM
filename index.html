Tipping Intelligence Layer

1 · Primary official evidence — VERY HIGH

NRL team lists, official late mail, judiciary, club injury updates, official withdrawals and confirmed final teams.

2 · Statistical evidence — HIGH

Season and recent-form performance, attack/defence profile, opponent-adjusted context, venue, rest and availability effects. Statistics inform probability rather than override current evidence.

3 · Specialist reporting — HIGH

Club reporters, credible local journalists, training observers and specialist Fantasy/SuperCoach late-mail reporters with demonstrable access or predictive reliability.

4 · Market intelligence — MEDIUM–HIGH

Opening/current head-to-head price, opening/current line, movement magnitude and velocity, cross-bookmaker agreement, timing relative to public news and any known explanatory event.

5 · Club/community intelligence — MEDIUM

Reserve-grade watchers, club-specific communities and supporter networks can expose promotions, withdrawals and role changes early. Corroboration is required before material weighting.

6 · Fantasy/SuperCoach intelligence — MEDIUM

Expected minutes, positional role, bench rotation, injury-return workload and reserve-list interpretation can reveal changes not obvious from headline team lists.

7 · Environmental intelligence — CONTEXT DEPENDENT

Weather, wind, humidity, surface, travel, turnaround, Origin backup and other match-specific conditions. Weight depends on a credible mechanism affecting the matchup.

8 · Weak social signals — LOW

Player/family posts, travel clues, deleted posts and anonymous claims are logged but remain UNVERIFIED unless independently corroborated. They cannot independently determine a MAYHEM tip.

9 · Officiating intelligence — LOW–MEDIUM

Referee tendencies are admitted only where statistically established. Folklore and unsupported referee narratives are rejected.

Evidence classification

VERIFIED

Supported by a sufficiently authoritative or independently corroborated source.

UNVERIFIED

Potentially relevant claim without enough support to treat as fact.

CONTRADICTED

Material claim conflicts with stronger evidence or subsequent confirmation.

SIGNAL

Observed movement or indicator worth tracking without claiming it establishes the underlying cause.

Decision architecture

Official evidence → statistical evidence → market intelligence → specialist reporting → club/community intelligence → weak signals → corroboration → contradiction analysis → confidence adjustment → MAYHEM TIP → evidence freeze → result → predictive-signal audit

Season ledger integrity

No retroactive tipping. The 2026 historical fixture/result ledger can be used for analysis, form and backtesting, but a match enters the live MAYHEM strike rate only if a selection was genuinely recorded before kickoff.

A retrospective model can be run separately, but it must remain labelled as a backtest and cannot contaminate the live tipping record.
