Information Advantage Audit

PUBLIC FRAGMENTS → AGGREGATION → INFERENCE → ADVANTAGE → DECISION → OUTCOME

This layer measures whether combining ordinary public information creates a measurable decision advantage beyond any individual source.

1 · Information availability
Record what was publicly knowable, its publication timestamp, source class, accessibility and whether it was broadly visible or fragmented across sources.
2 · Fragmentation
Record whether the signal was useful alone or only became meaningful after combination with other observations. Preserve the independent fragments.
3 · Aggregation and inference
Record the inference MAYHEM produced from the combined evidence, the supporting fragments, contradictions and confidence change. Inference must remain distinguishable from source fact.
4 · Market absorption
Compare the timing of the inferred signal with observable market movement. Determine whether the market appears to have already incorporated the information, incorporated it later, or showed no detectable response.
5 · Information advantage
Classify the apparent advantage as NONE, POSSIBLE, CORROBORATED or DEMONSTRATED. A correct result alone does not establish an information advantage.
6 · Decision effect
Record whether the intelligence changed the selected team, confidence, ranking or no decision at all. This separates interesting information from decision-useful information.
7 · Outcome validation
After the match, test the pre-match inference against the result and relevant match events without rewriting the frozen pre-match record.
8 · Source performance
Measure source classes longitudinally: frequency, corroboration rate, contradiction rate, decision influence and predictive contribution. Reweight only from accumulated evidence.
Core experiment: when does readily available information become powerful information because an actor can continuously collect, connect, timestamp and remember it?
